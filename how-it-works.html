PREPARATIONSTEP 01

Clarity makes
every handoff easier.

These workstreams deserve attention even when a potential mentor and student have already connected.

01 / LEARNING

A match in clinical objectives

The proposed site and preceptor should support the program’s intended patient population, educational goals and supervision expectations.

  • Credential and specialty scope
  • Clinical site and patient exposure
  • Student learning objectives
02 / SCHEDULING

A practical rotation plan

Both sides should discuss realistic dates, attendance and the volume of clinical hours expected before making a commitment.

  • Proposed start and end dates
  • Weekly schedule and availability
  • Clinical hour documentation
03 / APPROVAL

Formal school sign-off

Schools retain authority over accepting a preceptor or site. The approval process is a separate, essential part of every real placement.

  • Institutional forms and agreements
  • Site onboarding requirements
  • Written program approval
BEFORE YOU STARTPRACTICAL PREPARATION

Know what matters
before the first day.

Successful clinical rotations depend on a clear fit between the student, preceptor, clinical setting and academic program.

01 / YOUR PROGRAM

Understand school requirements.

Confirm required hours, eligible preceptor credentials and approval deadlines directly with your university.

02 / YOUR EXPERIENCE

Choose the right clinical setting.

Look for patient populations, supervision and learning opportunities that align with your training.

03 / YOUR NEXT STEP

Organize a clear request.

Share the specialty, preferred location, dates and other practical details a potential preceptor would need.
